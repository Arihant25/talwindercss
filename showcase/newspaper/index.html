Phase 3  ·  Central Assistance Cleared

Centre sanctions Rs 15,611 crore for Metro Phase 3, civil work to begin in March

Two elevated corridors of 44.65 km will run along the western arc of the Outer Ring Road and from Hosahalli to Kadabagere. BMRCL says land for 33 of the 41 stations has already been handed over.

By Anitha Raghunath  ·  Bengaluru, October 5

The Union Cabinet on Friday approved central assistance of Rs 15,611 crore for the third phase of the Bengaluru Metro, clearing a proposal that had been with the Ministry of Housing and Urban Affairs since November 2023.

The sanction covers two elevated corridors. Corridor 1 will run 32.15 km from J.P. Nagar to Kempapura along the western arc of the Outer Ring Road, with 21 stations. Corridor 2 will run 12.5 km from Hosahalli to Kadabagere, with nine stations. Both are to be completed by December 2031.

Bangalore Metro Rail Corporation Limited managing director M. Maheshwar Rao said tenders for the first four packages will be floated on 20 October and that civil work is expected to start in March. "The alignment drawings were revised twice after the city traffic police objected to the pier positions near Nagarbhavi Circle. That is settled now," he said at a briefing at the corporation office on Shanthinagar.

The state government's share of Rs 9,300 crore will be raised partly through a soft loan from the Japan International Cooperation Agency. The Department of Economic Affairs conveyed its concurrence to the borrowing on 12 September, a note placed before the Cabinet said.

Of the 41 stations planned across the two corridors, land for 33 has been handed over to the corporation. The Bruhat Bengaluru Mahanagara Palike has been asked to complete the transfer of the remaining eight parcels, all of them in Yeshwanthpur and Rajarajeshwarinagar, by 30 November.

Residents' associations along Magadi Road have asked for a review of the depot site at Kadabagere, where about 12 acres of a village tank bed falls inside the acquisition boundary. The Karnataka Tank Conservation and Development Authority is to record its opinion this week. A senior officer of the authority said the depot could be shifted 400 metres north without affecting the alignment.

Fares on the new corridors will be fixed by a fare fixation committee to be constituted after the corridors are commissioned. The corporation expects daily ridership on Phase 3 to reach 8.9 lakh by the third year of operation, against 8.3 lakh carried by the whole network at present.

Water

BWSSB proposes 15 paise rise in water tariff from December

By Staff Reporter

The Bangalore Water Supply and Sewerage Board has sent the government a proposal to raise the domestic tariff by 15 paise a litre from 1 December, the first revision since 2014.

A household using 8,000 litres a month in the lowest slab would pay about Rs 96 more. The board says its annual power bill for pumping from the Cauvery has risen to Rs 1,240 crore and that arrears from bulk consumers stand at Rs 388 crore.

The Urban Development Department will place the proposal before the Cabinet after a public objection period of 30 days. Objections may be filed at the board's Cauvery Bhavan office.

Weather Warning

Yellow alert for three coastal districts till Wednesday

By Our Correspondent  ·  Mangaluru

The India Meteorological Department has kept a yellow alert for Dakshina Kannada, Udupi and Uttara Kannada till Wednesday, with heavy rain likely at isolated places.

Mangaluru recorded 41.6 mm in the 24 hours ending 8.30 a.m. on Sunday. The deputy commissioner has asked schools in Sullia and Puttur taluks to keep the holiday declared on Friday under review.

Fishermen have been advised not to go to sea till Tuesday. The Karnataka State Disaster Management Authority has positioned two teams at Karwar and Kundapura.

Courts

High Court seeks report on 1,342 vacancies in district judiciary

By Legal Correspondent

A division bench of the High Court of Karnataka on Friday asked the Law Department to file a compliance report by 24 October on the filling of 1,342 sanctioned posts lying vacant in the district judiciary, including 214 posts of civil judge.

The bench was hearing a petition filed by the Advocates' Association, Bengaluru, which said recruitment notified in 2023 had not been completed.

The Additional Advocate General told the court that the written examination for 148 posts was held in July and that the results would be declared by the end of this month.

Editorial

The lessons Phase 2 left behind

The money for Phase 3 has been sanctioned faster than anyone in the corporation expected. That is the easy part. Phase 2 was sanctioned in 2014 and the Kalena Agrahara to Nagawara line is still not open. The delay was not caused by a shortage of funds. It was caused by land that was handed over in pieces, by three changes of contractor on the underground packages, and by drawings revised after work had started.

The corporation has said land for 33 of the 41 stations on the new corridors is already in hand. If the remaining eight parcels are not transferred by the end of November, the March start date will slip, and each month of slippage on an elevated corridor costs about Rs 41 crore in escalation.

The second thing to settle before work starts is the depot at Kadabagere. Objections about the tank bed are on record. They should be disposed of now, in writing, by the authority that is meant to decide them, and not left to a court in 2029.
